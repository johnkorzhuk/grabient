## 8. URL Inspection — the diagnosis inverts

Run after the permission upgrade to `siteFullUser` (which also unlocked sitemap
submission: `/sitemap.xml` submitted 2026-08-16, 0 errors, 0 warnings).

| URL | Coverage state |
|---|---|
| `/` | Submitted and indexed (crawled 08-16) |
| `/newest` | Submitted and indexed (07-30) |
| `/palettes/purple` | Submitted and indexed (08-05) |
| `/palettes/blue` | Submitted and indexed (07-30) |
| `/palettes/green` | Submitted and indexed (07-24) |
| `/palettes/sunset` | Submitted and indexed (**04-24** — four months stale) |
| seed sample (6 URLs) | 2 indexed · 3 "Discovered – currently not indexed" · 1 "URL is unknown to Google" |

**The color pages are already indexed and still earn zero clicks.** That is a
ranking problem, not a discovery problem, and it re-aims the work: titles,
on-page hex text, images and internal linking matter; sitemap listing alone will
not move them. The sitemap expansion is still correct (it gets the other ~20
colors crawled and gives GSC a per-family coverage report) but it is no longer
the primary lever for the six colors already listed.

**The seed corpus is mostly NOT indexed.** Two of the sampled six are in;
half are "Discovered – currently not indexed", which is Google's standard signal
for thin or templated pages it does not consider worth storing — exactly what
866 pages sharing one meta description and carrying no unique text would produce.
One of the two highest-traffic seed pages in GA4 (1,163 views) is literally
*unknown to Google*.

Consequences:
- Differentiating seed pages (coefficient-derived tags, a real `<img>`, related
  links, per-palette descriptions) is the only thing that will change this. It
  moves from "nice to have" to the precondition for 866 URLs being worth anything.
- The split sitemap earns its keep immediately: it will report indexed counts for
  the seed family separately, so this becomes a tracked number instead of a
  six-URL sample.

**Every URL was crawled as MOBILE.** Mobile-first indexing is in effect, so the
mobile rendering is the one that counts — and GA4 shows mobile at 21% of
impressions with a lower CTR. Worth attention once content work lands.

Crawl freshness is poor: `/palettes/sunset` was last crawled 2026-04-24. The
`<lastmod>` values shipping with the split sitemap are aimed exactly at this.
