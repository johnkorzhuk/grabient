# Turkish (tr)

How Turkish says Grabient's words. Read `README.md` first; use these words every time, and add a row when a new product
word gets its Turkish.

## How Turkish writes here

- Buttons, tabs, chips and menu items: the short imperative. Kopyala, İndir, Kaydet, Dışa aktar, Giriş yap, Tekrar dene.
- Sentences to the reader: polite and plain, the siz form. "Tekrar deneyin", "hesabınız", "Önce bir palet seçin."
- A tooltip is the press's name, as on its button, then its key: "Sırayı çevir · E".
- A group label phrased as a question takes the Ne / Hangi / Kimler form: "Ne dışa aktarılacak", "Kimler görebilir". Not a nominalized clause ("Neyin dışa aktarılacağı").
- Status lines: passive past. "Kaydedildi", "Palet silindi"; a failure is "-(y)AmAdI": "Kaydedilemedi", "Bağlantı gönderilemedi".
- Sentence case everywhere.
- No suffix is ever attached to a `{value}`: vowel harmony depends on the word, which the message cannot know. Build the
  sentence so the value stands alone: "{format} olarak kopyala", "{hex} kodunu kopyala", "{title} koleksiyonuna
  eklendi", "{number}. paleti kaldır", "{key} ile geri alın", or a colon: "Benzer paletler: {name}", "Kapatan: {name}".
- Palette names ("{start} to {end}"): "{start} ve {end}" ("Açık sarı ve pembe"), since "-dan ... -a" depends on the word.
  The name then works in every title: "Açık sarı ve pembe doğrusal gradyan", "Açık sarı ve pembe paleti".
- Plurals: Turkish has one and other; after a number the noun stays singular, so both cases usually read the same.
- Numbers: percent before the number ("%{percent}"); thousands with a dot ("2.048", "5.000"). OKLCH readings in the About
  page keep the dot decimal (0.86), as the figures and code beside them do.
- Abbreviations take the suffix of their Turkish letter names after an apostrophe (RGB'de, sRGB'nin, GA4'te, AB'de);
  where the reading is disputed (CSS, JSON, OKLCH), the sentence avoids the suffix ("CSS kodunu", "JSON olarak",
  "OKLCH uzayında").
- TDK spelling, with the circumflex where the dictionary has it: dâhil, hâl, zekâ, hikâye, kâr, şikâyet.
- Dotted and dotless i everywhere, including capitals: İndir, İptal, Iraksak.
- Search examples stay English where the words are the search vocabulary: "navy, cozy veya sunset". Signed out,
  the search reads only those English tags; a Turkish example would find nothing.
- A document title takes its suffix without an apostrophe, as law names do in Turkish legal writing (Türk Ceza
  Kanununun): "Gizlilik Politikasını", "Hizmet Koşullarını", as the sign-in line has them.
  The page name Ayarlar takes one: "Ayarlar'da", "Ayarlar'dan", as a tab's name does: "Yükselenler'e".
